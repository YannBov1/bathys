# Colour underwater: model and data provenance

This is a spectral teaching experiment, separate from the dive scenario and lake survey. It borrows the direct / forward-scatter / backscatter decomposition associated with Jaffe–McGlamery, and demonstrates the distinct broadband weights described by [Akkaynak & Treibitz, A Revised Underwater Image Formation Model (CVPR 2018)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/html/Akkaynak_A_Revised_Underwater_CVPR_2018_paper.html). It is **not** a numerical implementation of a complete Jaffe/McGlamery camera simulator, HydroLight, Sea-thru reconstruction, or a calibrated prediction of human underwater appearance. [Jaffe’s imaging-system discussion](https://tos.org/oceanography/article/underwater-optical-imaging-the-design-of-optimal-systems) explains the illumination/camera common-volume scattering problem.

## Offline optical tables

`water_spectra.dat` contains 61 samples, 400–700 nm inclusive at 5 nm, for six marine classes: IB, II, III, 1C, 3C, 5C. These are the six classes supported by the selected a/b source; other Jerlov classes were not invented or silently interpolated. They are not freshwater lake measurements.

`Tools/prepare_optics.py` parses the pinned zip/CSV/XLSX XML with the Python standard library, checks pinned SHA-256 values, compares every water-table cell to the original files and colour samples to official CIE data, and produces the compact offline table. Run `python Tools/prepare_optics.py` from the repository. Raw inputs are under `Tools/OpticsSource`; they are excluded from the web export. Source hashes are also recorded in the generated table. The derived table is approximately 14 KB; no source workbook, radiative-transfer database or external Python dependency is loaded by the app.

## Equations and assumptions

All calculations are spectral until display conversion. Water is homogeneous, the camera and target are at the same depth z, and their horizontal distance is r. Spectral surface irradiance is E₀. Radiometric values are relative. A chosen Lambertian target has synthetic reflectance ρ(λ); these are not measured ColorChecker spectra or inferred photo colours.

Ambient target irradiance is `E = E₀ exp(−Kd z)`. Beam attenuation is `c = a + b`. With no torch, object radiance before the return water path is `J = ρ E / π`; direct received light is `D = J exp(−c r)`. Thus the downward ambient path and the return viewing path are distinct, rather than one arbitrary depth-based RGB tint.

The normalized Henyey–Greenstein phase function is `p(μ) = (1−g²) / [4π (1+g²−2gμ)^(3/2)]`, where μ is the scattering-angle cosine and the integral over the sphere is 1. The phase plot uses log₁₀ p; the 0° direction is forward. Backward and accepted-cone fractions are solid-angle integrals, not phase values at one angle. Reduced scattering is `b′ = b(1−g)`; it is not substituted for Kd or beam c.

For the ambient veil, a fraction f of irradiance is assigned to a uniform downwelling hemisphere and the remainder to a direct overhead beam. For horizontal viewing the chosen source per path length is `S = b E [(1−f) p(90°) + f/(2π)]`. This yields `B(r) = (S/c)[1−exp(−c r)]` and `B∞ = S/c`. This is a homogeneous source-term approximation, not a measured underwater angular light field. Kd/a/b class combinations and angular assumptions are illustrative and need not form an exactly self-consistent radiative-transfer solution.

The torch is a D65 point-source approximation aimed at the target, with Gaussian angular σ = 15°, inverse-square spreading and a finite 0.25 m near-field cutoff. Its strength is relative to surface daylight at 1 m before water attenuation. An offset d from the camera changes lamp-to-target distance `L = sqrt(r²+d²)`. Target irradiance includes `power exp(−cL) max(L²,0.25²)⁻¹ r/L`. The camera return attenuation remains exp(−cr). Torch backscatter is a 48-midpoint single-scattering integral along the camera line of sight, with lamp-to-particle and particle-to-camera attenuation, beam overlap, b and HG scattering angle. Offset and beam overlap matter, so this is more than a second Beer–Lambert tint. It omits beam occlusion, housing optics, near-field source shape and multiple scattering.

The optional recovered forward term is `F = J exp(−ar)[1−exp(−br)] P_HG(θ≤α)`. This ensures D+F ≤ J exp(−ar) for a uniform target. It is an explicitly chosen **energy-bounded surrogate**, not the exact scattered path energy or a measured camera PSF. A normalized nine-tap Gaussian-like kernel blurs F only; D stays sharp. Its width uses the accepted-cone HG angular second moment, uniformly distributed scattering positions, a 2 m wide target plane and the fixed 2.2 preview aspect ratio. The metric signal budget refers to a uniform white target, not an exact average of the finite chart’s white patch. At b(550 nm)r > 3 the UI adds a qualitative-preview note; this is a teaching indicator, not a universal validity threshold.

For the third stage, the torch is excluded and each spectrum is integrated first with positive CIE Y weights: `D_Y = Σ Dλ ȳλ Δλ`, with analogous J_Y, B_Y and B∞_Y. Then `βD = −ln(D_Y/J_Y)/r` and `βB = −ln(1−B_Y/B∞_Y)/r`. White and red reflectances give different direct weights, while the veil has its own source/c weights. The resulting effective coefficients depend on range/illumination/target response. They illustrate the revised model’s distinction; **they are not camera RGB coefficients or a full reimplementation of the paper’s acquisition/calibration method**. Zero range has undefined fitted coefficients and is explicitly handled.

Display integrates the CIE observer to XYZ, converts to linear sRGB, sums light components before the display transfer function, applies the chosen exposure and clips to gamut. Surface reference remains fixed. There is no auto exposure, white balance, local tone mapping or human adaptation. Negative out-of-gamut linear components are not used as attenuation-fit channels; CIE Y diagnostics avoid that issue. Exact reproduction also depends on the display.

## Other supplied sources

[IOCCG Report 5](https://ioccg.org/what-we-do/ioccg-publications/ioccg-reports/synthesized-dataset-from-ioccg-report-5/) and the [Dryad hyperspectral ocean optics database](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.6076/D1630T) were reviewed as richer radiative-transfer references. Their synthetic IOP/AOP scenarios are not mixed into the compact Jerlov presets; the large HydroLight database was not bundled. The [NASA optics protocols](https://seabass.gsfc.nasa.gov/wiki/System_Description/Protocols_Ver4_VolIV.pdf) provide absorption/optical-property context. Pure-water absorption is not added again to a total-a spectrum. The supplied ephemeral photo links could not be fetched, and no photograph is used as quantitative reflectance truth. Synthetic targets provide controlled starting spectra and detail instead.

Excluded effects include a full multiple-scattering radiative-transfer solution, wavelength redistribution/fluorescence/Raman scattering, depth stratification, bottom/environment reflections, surface waves, polarized light, lens/sensor response/noise and visual adaptation. The lesson makes these approximations visible rather than treating an illustrative image as an observation.
