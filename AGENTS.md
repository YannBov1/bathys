# Lesson design preferences

Preserve the visual-first teaching style requested by the user. Start with an intuitive, interactive scene; prefer dragging the object or manipulating a recognizable instrument over a wall of sliders and text. Use semantic controls (thermometer, clock, salt shaker, distance ruler, segmented O₂/N₂/He tank), external drag cues that do not cover the subject, and short contextual feedback. Linked charts start enabled. Keep explanations, equations, model limits and sources available through the information toggle. Use visual instruments as the only parameter interface, and show all instruments relevant to the current stage by default; do not add a precise-values drawer or an instrument-picker toggle. Separate distinct formulas with whitespace or separate lines, reserving multiplication dots for mathematical products. Keep English and French, touch, keyboard controls and narrow layouts working. Preserve scientific assumptions and the separation between teaching experiments and the dive plan. Document substantive changes and verify interactions and rendered layouts.
